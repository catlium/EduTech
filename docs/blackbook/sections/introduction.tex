\chapter{INTRODUCTION}
\thispagestyle{empty}

This chapter introduces the fragmented content-to-examination workflow
faced by educational institutions, presents the CatLium EduTech platform
proposed to address it, and states the project's problem statement,
motivation, objectives, scope, and significance.

\clearpage

\section{Background}

Educational institutions increasingly rely on digital tools to manage
teaching material, assessments, and examinations. However, the workflow from
raw content to a delivered examination remains fragmented. Syllabi must be
structured manually into teaching hierarchies, printed study material must
be scanned and converted into machine-readable text, question sets are
authored by hand, and examination papers are assembled, scheduled, attempted,
and evaluated across separate systems. For institutions that serve large
numbers of students this manual cycle is slow, error-prone, and difficult to
scale.

CatLium EduTech is a multi-tenant Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform that
automates this complete lifecycle. A teacher creates a subject, uploads a
syllabus, and an AI assistant proposes a chapter--topic hierarchy for review
and confirmation. Study materials---PDFs, documents, images, or plain
text---are processed through an OCR pipeline so that scanned and printed
documents become searchable text. A deterministic Material Intelligence
engine cleans, structures, and segments the extracted text and maps each
logical region to the relevant syllabus entities. From such structured
content the platform extracts examination paper patterns and question sets,
populates an AI-assisted question bank, assembles question papers that
conform to a pattern, schedules assessments, and lets students attempt them
with server-side deadline enforcement and deterministic evaluation.

This black book documents the completed implementation: its analysis,
requirements, design, implementation, testing, and results, based on the
approved project proposal and the actual state of the repository.

\section{Problem Statement}

Institutions must convert unstructured academic input (syllabus documents,
printed study material, past question papers) into structured academic
assets (a confirmed teaching hierarchy, searchable materials, a curated
question bank, conforming examination papers) and then run the examination
lifecycle (scheduling, attempts, evaluation, results). Doing this manually
is slow and inconsistent; off-the-shelf learning management systems provide
parts of the lifecycle but rarely connect raw-document processing, AI-assisted
generation, and deterministic examination management into a single tenant-safe
workflow.

\section{Motivation}

The motivation for the project is to eliminate the manual, disconnected steps
in the content-to-examination pipeline while keeping the teacher as the final
authority. AI is used only to propose and assist, never to write authoritative
structure without review. Deterministic, rule-based engines (OCR
normalization, pattern and question extraction, grading of objective
questions) are preferred wherever correctness matters, and every component is
multi-tenant so that independent institutes can be served from one
deployment without data leakage.

\section{Project Overview}

The platform is a modular monolith. A single NestJS (TypeScript) API hosts the
business domains---identity, tenancy, academic structure, content, materials,
OCR coordination, Material Intelligence, questions, paper patterns, question
papers, examinations, attempts, practice, and export. Independent Python
processes provide a separately deployable FastAPI OCR service and asynchronous
workers (material processing and AI generation) that consume RabbitMQ queues.
A Next.js web application provides the teacher and student interfaces. All
components run in a single Docker Compose stack behind a Cloudflare Tunnel,
which is the only public ingress, with PostgreSQL as the primary database.

\section{Objectives}

The project objectives, as approved in the proposal, are to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item design a multi-tenant SaaS architecture with strict tenant isolation
        and role based access control for administrators, teachers, and
        students;
  \item automate syllabus structuring through an AI assistant that proposes a
        chapter--topic hierarchy, made authoritative only after explicit
        teacher confirmation;
  \item build an OCR processing pipeline that converts PDF and image study
        material into searchable text with page-level fidelity, distributed
        workers, chunked uploads, and incremental page reveal;
  \item implement Material Intelligence, which cleans, structures, and
        segments raw extraction and maps each region to syllabus entities;
  \item enable deterministic pattern and question extraction from source
        documents into reviewable forms with explicit ambiguity reports;
  \item provide an AI-assisted question bank that satisfies paper-pattern
        structure and coverage rules;
  \item deliver the examination and practice lifecycle, including scheduled
        assessments, attempts with immutable question snapshots and deadline
        enforcement, deterministic evaluation of objective question types,
        results, and ungraded practice;
  \item build a complete web application with validated design states for
        every asynchronous operation;
  \item validate continuously with unit test suites, linting, type checking,
        end-to-end harnesses, and live container rebuild-and-verify
        discipline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope}

\subsection{Implemented scope}

The implemented platform includes identity and tenancy (cookie-based session
authentication with CSRF protection, institutes, memberships, role based
authorization), academic structure with AI-proposed and teacher-confirmed
syllabus, materials and distributed OCR with retries and cancellation,
Material Intelligence with versioning and syllabus mapping, AI-generated study
content (notes, summaries, flashcards, concepts), the question domain with
deterministic extraction, paper patterns with validation and locking, question
papers and assessments with export to PDF, DOCX, and Excel, examinations and
attempts with deterministic evaluation, and ungraded practice.

\subsection{Excluded and deferred scope}

The following are explicitly out of scope and not implemented: online
answer-sheet capture, optical mark recognition, and on-screen marking;
automatic grading of text or essay questions (answers are stored but never
graded); practice scoring and attempt-based (N-of-M) mechanics with
replenishment; question versioning and question-set delete/merge; and the
redesigned per-topic, per-resource academic export.

\section{Significance}

The significant contribution of the project is an integrated, evidence-backed
pipeline that connects syllabus to examination in one tenant-safe platform, in
which every AI contribution is validated against canonical schemas and
reviewed by a human, every grading and extraction decision is deterministic,
and the entire system is documented and validated continuously so that
development can be resumed from any checkpoint.
